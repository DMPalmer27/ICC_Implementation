  Where the interior term binds, slack is 0.500 bits at H_2 regardless of f:
  the exact Gaussian value is f + log2(2*sqrt(pi)) = f + 1.825 against a bound
  of f + log2(sqrt(2*pi)). That is tight enough to use in place of an entropy
  estimate -- which the case study cannot produce anyway, since 25 samples
  cannot resolve a 2^18-ary distribution.

  The saturation term is the trap. At clip = 4 sigma, f = 16 the exact
  H_8 = 16.93 falls BELOW the interior term f + 1.325 = 17.33, because the
  clipped tail atom has overtaken a typical interior cell. Ignoring the
  saturation term there would have over-estimated H_p(X) and under-estimated m,
  i.e. failed in the unsafe direction. Rule: keep P_sat <= 2^-f * M.

    f   min clip (sigma) keeping the interior term binding
    8                                                 2.96
   12                                                 3.73
   16                                                 4.37
   20                                                 4.95
   24                                                 5.46
  Widening the clip is cheap: it enters the magnitude bound polynomially while
  the tail mass decays like a Gaussian. At n_s=25, P=3, f_X=8, f_w=0, going from
  4 to 5 sigma costs 0.94 bits of q and buys 6.79 bits of entropy headroom.
  So widen the clip, do not weaken the bound.

  h_i = min(H_2(symbol), log2 q_i) / log2 q_i to within numerical display:
  entropy in bits is capped by the source, so shrinking the field raises the
  ratio. Design rule: pick each CRT prime with log2 q_i <= H_p(symbol) in bits
  and h_i = 1, giving m_i >= r + p + log_{q_i}(1/eps), independent of n.
  Both Theorem 1 entropy terms then become tight and assumption-free
  (H_p(X) = n, max_R = r), which also sidesteps the correlated-features problem.
  Correctness is immediate (reduction mod q_i is a ring homomorphism); the
  privacy composition across the L instances is NOT -- docs/PLAN.md question 5.
